Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous are the Mesozoic periods in order. Early dinosaur relatives and mammals occurred in Triassic settings. Jurassic dinosaurs diversified, and early birds appeared. Cretaceous communities included varied dinosaurs, birds, flying reptiles and abundant flowering plants. Marine reptiles and other sea life belong to the same history but are not all dinosaurs.

The supplied reconstructions show different land and marine communities. Dinosaur hip categories, diets and locomotion varied; not every dinosaur was a large predator. Mammals already existed during the Mesozoic, even though their later prominence gives the Cenozoic its age-of-mammals label. Dominance is not the same event as origin.

Cenozoic mammals diversified into many habitats, including ancestors of bats, whales, primates and hoofed animals. Fossils of bones and teeth document changes within lineages, such as horses. This is a branching history of populations; it is not one adult animal transforming into another while the animation runs.

Late Cenozoic ice-age communities included mammoths and other large mammals, many of which later disappeared.
