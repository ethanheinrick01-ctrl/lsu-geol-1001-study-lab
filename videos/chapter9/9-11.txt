Layered stromatolites preserve the constructions of microbial communities. The source describes examples around three point five billion years old and compares them with modern Australian stromatolites. Photosynthetic cyanobacteria helped transform the environment by supplying oxygen. Modern analogues clarify the structure without making today’s atmosphere identical to the ancient one.

Around the source’s Cambrian boundary, diverse animals with hard coverings became widespread over millions of years. Trilobites, brachiopods and unusually preserved soft-bodied animals document this change. The Cambrian explosion is biological diversification on a geological timescale. It does not mean life first appeared then or that the event was an eruption.

Early Paleozoic seas contained corals, crinoids, mollusks and trilobites. Later, fishes became diverse while plants and insects occupied land. The artist’s source reconstructions represent interpretations of fossil evidence, rather than photographs of a vanished ecosystem. Compare the organisms and their environments instead of treating one scene as the whole era.

Amphibians and early reptiles diversified during later Paleozoic time alongside land plants, insects and marine life. The era ended with the Great Dying, a major extinction affecting oceans and land. The course describes environmental stresses and several proposed causes; no single illustrative animation establishes which causal combination produced the extinction.
