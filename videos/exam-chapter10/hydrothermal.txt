Cold seawater enters fractures in oceanic crust above a magma chamber. Heat drives circulation: the water warms, reacts with rock, and carries dissolved metals upward. At the seafloor, the hot fluid mixes with cold seawater. Tiny sulfide minerals precipitate, producing the dark cloud and building a chimney. The cloud is mineral particles, not combustion smoke. Chemical energy supports the vent community, even where sunlight cannot reach.
