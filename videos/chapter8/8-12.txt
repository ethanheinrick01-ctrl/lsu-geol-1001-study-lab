Joint sets divide rock into blocks and provide pathways along which weathering can act. Their orientation can influence cliffs, pillars, notches, and drainage. Recognize the fracture patterns in an outcrop before attributing them to one particular stress history.

Faults can produce aligned notches, scarps, crushed angular rock, displaced layers, and striations. Several observations are more persuasive than a straight valley alone. A scarp shows relative topographic offset but does not by itself identify the complete fault history.

Resistant tilted beds can support ridges and dip slopes while weaker units erode into lower ground. A ridge does not automatically mark an anticline, and a valley does not automatically mark a syncline. Follow rock layers and ages through the landscape.

Foliation, cleavage, and resistant folded layers can influence the shapes of metamorphic outcrops. Modern topography is a product of both structure and erosion. Distinguish a visible rock fabric from a hypothesis about when it formed.
