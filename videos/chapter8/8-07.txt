For suitable shale-rich parent material, increasing metamorphic grade can produce slate, then phyllite, schist, and gneiss. Slate is dull and fine grained, phyllite has a sheen, schist has visible aligned minerals, and gneiss commonly displays compositional bands.

Quartz-rich sandstone can become quartzite as quartz grains recrystallize and interlock; fractures may cut across grains. Limestone can become marble, a rock of recrystallized carbonate minerals. Both can have weak foliation, so use composition and texture together.

Mafic volcanic rocks can produce greenstone or other metamorphic rocks as mineral assemblages change. Felsic volcanic and plutonic rocks have different compositions and can form different products. A metamorphic name depends on the rock and its observable features, not grade alone.

Hornfels commonly forms when rock is heated near an intrusion. Gneissic banding can form in several parent compositions, and the parent of a particular gneiss may remain uncertain. Mineralogy, grain size, fabric, and field context jointly support identification.
