Rock cleavage is a tendency to split along closely spaced surfaces produced by deformation and mineral alignment. It may cut across the original bedding. Slate is a fine-grained rock that commonly has well-developed cleavage.

Foliation is a planar fabric expressed by aligned minerals or compositional layers. Some rocks split into thin sheets, while others show coarse light and dark bands. Distinguish this metamorphic fabric from depositional bedding that may still be visible.

Lineation is a linear fabric: elongated minerals, stretched objects, or intersections of planar features align along a direction. It is not the same geometric feature as a planar foliation. Different lineations reflect different processes and should be described before interpretation.

Relict bedding, clasts, and other features can survive metamorphism and record the parent rock. Minerals can rotate, grow, and become elongated during deformation. Compare these younger fabrics with preserved older structures rather than assuming all lines are bedding.
