A joint is a crack with little or no shear displacement along its surface. A fault is a fracture across which rocks have slipped. Follow a matching bed or other marker across the break before deciding whether movement occurred.

Joints can open as rocks cool and contract, as overlying rock is removed and pressure decreases, or under tectonic stresses. Columnar joints form during cooling. Sheet-like unloading joints follow reduced confinement as deeply formed rock approaches the surface.

An opening-mode joint is oriented approximately perpendicular to the direction in which the rock is pulled apart. Joint sets can divide an outcrop into regular blocks. Identify the opening direction and the visible planes before deciding which stresses acted.

Differential stress can produce a fracture along which blocks slip. The fault orientation depends on the full stress field, including stresses in the other directions. An observed fracture alone does not establish the amount or sense of motion without a displaced marker or other evidence.
