Heat, pressure, differential stress, and chemically active fluids can change rock minerals or texture. Burial, tectonic activity, nearby magma, and fluid circulation provide different combinations of those drivers. Metamorphism and deformation may occur together or independently.

Recrystallization can enlarge existing mineral crystals. Neocrystallization forms new minerals as reactions make a different assemblage stable. These terms describe different changes; neither means the whole rock must melt and freeze again.

Material can dissolve at stressed grain contacts and move in fluids. Chemical components can be redistributed, producing different mineral-rich layers. Whether the bulk composition changes depends on movement into or out of the rock, which must be supported by evidence.

During deformation, minerals and objects can rotate, flatten, stretch, or form aligned fabrics. Metamorphism can preserve older features as relicts while adding new minerals and foliation. Read the sequence of overprinting rather than assuming all features formed simultaneously.
