Faults, joints, folds, tilted layers, and shear zones can form in related systems. A fault propagating upward can bend layers above it, and slip over a bend in a thrust fault can fold the overlying rocks. Structural associations must be supported by geometry.

Compression can move thrust sheets over one another and shorten a region. Beds in and above the sheets may form anticlines and synclines. Related joints, smaller faults, and metamorphic fabrics can occur, but not every nearby structure must share the same age.

Cleavage in a fold commonly lies roughly parallel to the axial surface. It cuts bedding at different angles on the limbs and may be nearly perpendicular to bedding near a hinge in the supplied example. Trace both surfaces across the fold.

A younger fault can cut an older fold, and an older fault can itself be folded. Use cross-cutting, overprinting, and the geometry of displaced markers to order events. Proximity alone does not prove two structures formed together.
