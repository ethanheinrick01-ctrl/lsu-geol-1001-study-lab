New England contains different rock belts, faults, folds, and metamorphic rocks. The supplied map and cross section show structures at the surface and interpreted relations below it. Use the legend and orientation before comparing locations.

Paleozoic collisions contributed to Appalachian deformation and metamorphism. Later Mesozoic extension opened rift basins as North America separated from Africa. These are separate events; a modern map can display structures inherited from both.

The source map shows the highest metamorphic conditions in the central part of its illustrated area, decreasing toward both east and west. Grade is constrained using mineral assemblages and textures, not topographic elevation alone.

In the supplied regional interpretation, rocks in eastern Vermont were buried and then heated, while rocks in New Hampshire were initially heated and then buried. These paths are evidence-based reconstructions for specific locations, not a universal order for all mountain belts.
