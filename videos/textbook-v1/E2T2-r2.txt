Physical weathering breaks rock into pieces. Chemical weathering changes minerals: feldspar can react to form clay and dissolved products. These processes work together, but weathering is different from carrying the products away.

Transport abrades clasts and sorts them. Watch rounded grains move with the current, then settle as flow weakens. Larger grains commonly settle before smaller ones. Grain size, shape, and sorting record different parts of this history.

Burial pushes grains closer together and reduces pore space. Minerals then precipitate between grains and bind them. Compaction packs; cementation binds. Together they contribute to lithification. Sedimentary rock forms here without requiring the grains to melt.
