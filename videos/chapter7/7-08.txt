Breccia contains angular large clasts in a finer matrix and is commonly poorly sorted. Massive lenses or wedges may have little visible bedding because material arrived in a sudden chaotic event. Some fragments remain shattered but close together. These observations support limited rounding, but do not identify one unique environment.

Mudflows and debris flows carry mixtures down steep slopes. Landslides shatter rock during movement. Streams can build coarse alluvial fans at a mountain front, and glaciers can leave mixtures of large fragments and fine rock flour. Breccia is compatible with several of these settings, so inspect matrix, geometry and surrounding deposits.

Conglomerate has rounded gravel-sized clasts in sand or mud. Some beds have many touching clasts and little matrix; others contain scattered gravel within finer material. Rounding usually records abrasion and reworking. Smaller gravel can reflect weaker flow, a finer source, long abrasion or a combination of those factors.

Energetic streams and braided channels can deposit coarse gravel. Repeated wave pounding can round beach stones without moving them a great distance away. Rounded clasts therefore do not supply one exact travel distance. Match the rock with channel forms, shoreline evidence and other observations before choosing its environment.

Conglomerate can also occur in deltas and underwater flows near continental slopes. Large clasts show that an effective transporting or moving process was available; the setting need not have been dry land. Breccia and conglomerate share coarse grain size, while angular versus rounded clasts distinguish their names.
