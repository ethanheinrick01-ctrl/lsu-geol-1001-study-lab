Continental maps are snapshots of an evolving system, not photographs of the distant past. This textbook presents one reconstruction beginning around six hundred million years ago with Rodinia and its breakup. Its exact arrangement, including the neighbor once west of North America, remains uncertain. Keep those source dates and uncertainties attached to the sequence rather than treating every drawn coastline as precisely known.

By five hundred million years ago, North America and Europe were separate, while Gondwana grouped the southern landmasses, including Africa, South America, Antarctica, Australia, and India. Most of the rest of Earth was occupied by a vast ocean. The modern continental names help identify pieces, but their positions and orientations were very different.

Around three hundred seventy million years ago, the reconstruction shows North America joined with parts of Europe and approaching Gondwana. Collisions narrowed intervening oceans and built mountains. By the late Paleozoic snapshot around two hundred eighty million years ago, the continents were assembled into Pangaea. The Appalachians and Ouachitas record parts of that assembly. The Tethys remained a major oceanic embayment.

By the one-hundred-fifty-million-year snapshot, the Atlantic had begun separating North America from Africa and South America. North America remained connected with Europe and Asia in Laurasia. Much of Gondwana was still joined, but further rifting would separate South America from Africa and disperse its other pieces. Spreading creates new oceanic crust between moving continents; it does not push them through older ocean floor.

The present arrangement is another snapshot. Work backward using matching rock histories and plate relationships, then forward through the same sequence. The opening ocean and closing ocean are linked to continental motions and mountain belts. Future arrangements require assumptions about how boundaries evolve; today's directions alone cannot justify an exact distant-future map. Here, motion between supplied snapshots is a teaching schematic, not a measured trajectory.
