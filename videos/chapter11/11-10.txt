North America did not form in one event. Its oldest exposed crust includes the roughly four-billion-year-old Acasta Gneiss, while much of the Canadian Shield contains younger Precambrian rocks. Successive additions enlarged that core. A continent's age therefore depends on what you mean: the age of a rock, the time a terrane attached, or the age of sediment covering older basement.

Precambrian provinces were added along the southern core, including the Grenville region farther southeast. Paleozoic collisions added eastern terranes, and western additions continued through later time. Younger sediment covers much older basement across the continental platform and coastal plain. A young color on a surface-geology map does not mean the whole crust underneath formed recently. Southern Florida even conceals a fragment associated with Africa.

Other continents show the same broad ingredients in different arrangements. Western Australia contains an ancient core, with younger additions eastward. Africa assembled several Precambrian shields. Much of Asia joined through Paleozoic and Mesozoic accretion around older fragments. India preserves ancient crust carried away during Gondwana's breakup. Eastern South America's older regions once connected with Africa, while younger construction shaped much of its western side.

California exposes oceanic crust and sediment, island-arc material, and an accretionary prism assembled along a convergent margin. Those imported pieces coexist with rocks formed locally, including Sierra Nevada intrusions and later volcanic cover. The terrane map specifically omits some units formed in place. Read its purpose and legend before assuming every missing rock is absent from the region.

Continents grow by additions, lose pieces through rifting, shorten internally, stretch, and shift along strike-slip faults. Deposition can bury that assembly without creating an entirely new continent. When reading an age map, ask whether each pattern records basement formation, tectonic attachment, or younger cover. That distinction turns a patchwork of colors into a history you can explain.
