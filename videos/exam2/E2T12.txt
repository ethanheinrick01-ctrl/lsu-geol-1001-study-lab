Compare tectonic motion
Extension can produce normal faults, down-dropped grabens, and raised horsts. Shortening produces folds and thrusts. Sideways shear produces strike-slip displacement. Watch marker layers to define relative movement.

Expose the structures
Erosion exposes resistant inclined layers and weaker fractured zones. A dip slope follows bedding. A ridge is not automatically an anticline: resistance and erosion shape landscapes. Bedding and later cleavage can differ.

Reconstruct the sequence
A fault is younger than the layers it cuts. A continuous unit sealing the break is younger than the observed slip. New England records collision and metamorphism, then later rifting. Combine maps, fabrics, and cross-cutting relations; one rock name cannot establish every event.
