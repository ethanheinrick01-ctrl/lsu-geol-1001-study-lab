Coarse clasts
Begin with grain size. Gravel-sized fragments can form breccia or conglomerate. Angular pieces identify breccia. As transport abrades corners, rounded pieces can form conglomerate. Rounding does not establish one unique environment.

Sand-sized grains
Sand accumulates and becomes sandstone as grains are compacted and cemented. Mineral composition refines the name: feldspar-rich sandstone is arkose. Well-sorted quartz sand differs from sand with abundant matrix or less resistant minerals.

Fine sediment
Mud settles in quieter conditions and can become mudstone. Shale is a mudrock that splits along thin layers. To infer the setting, add fossils, bedding, and regional relations. Grain size is a starting observation, not a complete environmental history.
