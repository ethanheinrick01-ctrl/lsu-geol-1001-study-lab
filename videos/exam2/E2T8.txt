A break without slip
A joint is a fracture without appreciable displacement along its surface. Cooling, unloading, or stress can create joints. A fault requires relative displacement. Trace a marker layer across the break.

Normal and reverse
The hanging wall lies above this inclined plane. Move it down relative to the footwall: a normal fault, commonly associated with extension. Move it up: a reverse fault, commonly associated with shortening. A thrust is a low-angle reverse fault.

Sideways movement
In map view, the blocks slide horizontally. Matching markers become offset across a strike-slip fault. Read relative motion; cliff height alone does not identify the fault type.
