Build carbonate
Organisms make shells and skeletons that accumulate as carbonate sediment. Warm, clear, shallow water can support reefs. Tiny carbonate skeletons can form chalk; recognizable shell fragments can form coquina. Both differ from clastic quartz sand.

Roll and coat grains
Agitated shallow water rolls grains while carbonate precipitates around them. Repeated coatings form ooids. Watch the coatings grow around a moving nucleus. Magnesium-bearing fluids can later change carbonate mineralogy to dolomite.

Evaporate or preserve
As saline water evaporates, dissolved minerals can precipitate as evaporites. In a saturated wetland, plant material can survive decay and become coal through burial. Identify composition and formation pathway together; nonclastic rocks do not share one single origin.
